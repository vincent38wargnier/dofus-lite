## AI art production
Keep one approved art direction, palette, light direction and character reference throughout the package. Create one image per requested filename, not a labelled contact sheet. Generate each distinct class/creature once; duplicate enemies may share its artwork. Include clothing in the character art: PNG actors do not have automatic hat/cape/pet attachment points. A larger boss uses the SAME 256x256 frames and foot anchor; do not enlarge its canvas.
Character/creature artwork and 64x64 spell icons are importable today. The legacy map-layer guides, equipment PNGs, cover images and arbitrary spell PNG/audio sequences are DESIGN SOURCES. New flat map geometry/backgrounds can now travel in atelier.json via battlefield; see references/original-battlefields.md. content.json still uses an existing map ID. Both formats accept the original vector visualEffect descriptor described in the runtime rules. Deliver only the unsupported design sources separately in a design-sources folder/ZIP; do not put undeclared files inside the playable ZIP. Equipment stats and built-in shape/color appearances CAN travel in an Atelier export; this does not enable arbitrary equipment PNGs. New terrain/collision/placement rules cannot be created by drawing a map.
For the legacy map-layer design workflow, choose an existing map ID and use its exact guide from the complete kit (assets/ai-design/maps/<mapId>/). Attach guide.png, clean.png and geometry.json to the image AI; preserve projection, canvas, traversable cells, LoS and spawn positions. The overlay is technical guidance, never part of the final picture. Full map and layer prompts are included below. Without the guide files, produce a labelled concept only and retain the original playable map. Do not invent an import field for it.
AI images may have wrong sizes, painted checkerboards, clipped silhouettes, extra frames or inconsistent limbs. Inspect EVERY animation frame and verify real alpha, dimensions, filenames and file limits before packing. Blank guides are drawing aids and must never be presented as finished theme art. If the AI has no image tools, omit optional artwork fields and state that native fallbacks are used; do not fabricate PNGs, downloads or completed image work.
The complete kit includes an offline visual guide, one prompt per output, character SVG/blank PNG templates, icon/equipment templates, map guides/geometry and a local PNG checker. These authoring resources are not files to put in content.json's playable ZIP.
Raster spell animations have a complete DESIGN-source kit in assets/ai-design/spell-fx/: a closed effect.json schema, six layer templates, an offline animated viewer and a portable checker (scripts/check-spell-fx.cjs). Use the complete effect and per-layer prompts below. These conventions are new authoring rules, not the native SWF format, and are not yet imported by the game.

### Direction artistique commune — reference
THEME / CE QUE JE VEUX CREER: [describe the project].
Plan a consistent visual identity for a tactical RPG class, dungeon and equipment collection. Deliver a short art bible with palette (hex colours), light direction, materials, silhouette language, readable motifs, and the difference between hero, companion and boss. Keep the UI's elemental colour cues legible.
Create an asset checklist: each distinct character/creature needs 3 core PNGs and up to 6 animation PNGs; each distinct spell may have one 64x64 icon. The map-layer guide outputs, equipment art, covers and arbitrary PNG FX/audio remain design sources. Original flat battlefields and vector effects have a separate runtime contract in references/original-battlefields.md; use that contract when original playable scenery/effects are required.
For every entry specify owner key, exact filename, size, frame count, input references, prompt, current import support and review status (planned / generated / checked). Do not mark a file generated until it exists. Do not change combat mechanics to match the art. Start by approving a front idle and palette; reuse them for all other views. Reference images and descriptions are not sprites.

Use the exact schema below. Times are integer milliseconds; duration 100..2000 ms, normally 1500. Frames may be 1..16, fps 8..60, each frame 256x256, sheet width=frames*256. Scale is in percent. Anchors are pixels from the TOP LEFT of each frame. All fields are required; no unrecognised properties.

All windows fit durationMs. For travel none, startMs=endMs=arcHeightPercent=0. Line travel has arcHeightPercent=0; arc is screen-up quadratic curvature, height=distance*arcHeightPercent/100. Projectile times exactly match travel. Trail emits one stationary particle every 50 ms during travel; it starts with travel and endMs must be at least travel.endMs+frames/fps*1000 so the last particles can fade. Impact begins at or after arrival. A beam uses no travel, rotation=path, anchor (0,128).

### Impact — impact.png
One readable impact/explosion, centred at (128,128). Eight successive frames: initial contact, compressed flash, expansion, peak silhouette, fragments, fading energy, residual particles, faint final residue. One shot, at least 334 ms. Final frame remains nonempty. The anchor never moves; intensity expands around it. No screen-wide flash or obscuring the whole battlefield.

### Aura / soin / protection — aura.png

Keep a clear centre so the actor remains readable. This is a short visual cue, not the duration or hit area of a gameplay buff.

### Rayon — beam.png
A horizontal beam from the LEFT edge midpoint (0,128) towards (256,128), eight-frame energy loop. It is stretched only along X and rotated by the viewer to join caster and target. Keep width/thickness stable, no circles or complex symbols that become distorted. No baked endpoint explosion; use impact.png separately. Unlike other parts, its anchor is (0,128).
